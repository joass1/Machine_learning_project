**How the stats connect to the project.** The ACSI 2026 result that in-flight internet ratings rose 20% (M2), and the IATA GPS finding that passengers want digital, mobile-first journeys (M11), match the drivers that earlier ML studies on this dataset found most important: in-flight Wi-Fi and online boarding ([22], [24], [25]). Our SHAP analysis can test whether the same drivers appear in the synthetic Playground data.

**Draft motivation paragraph** (citations placed where each number appears):
> Air travel is at record scale: IATA expects 5.1 billion passengers in 2026 [4], but net profit is forecast at only US$4.5 per departing passenger [3]. Satisfaction is therefore a lever on thin margins. U.S. airline satisfaction reached an ACSI score of 76 in 2026, with the largest gains in technology-enabled services such as in-flight internet (+20%) [1]. Satisfaction predicts repurchase and recommendation intentions among airline passengers [12] and is linked to shareholder value [9], [10], and retaining customers is far cheaper than acquiring them [8].

### 2.2 The original dataset and its provenance

Kaggle API metadata, fetched 2026-10-07:

- **Dataset [19]:** "Airline Passenger Satisfaction" by **TJ Klein** (`teejmahal20`), version 1, last updated **2020-02-20**, about 15.2 MB, licence "Other (specified in description)", 141,551 downloads at time of check. URL: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/teejmahal20/airline-passenger-satisfaction
- **Stated provenance (quote):** "Note that this data set was modified from this dataset by John D [here](https://www.kaggle.com/johndddddd/customer-satisfaction). It has been cleaned up for the purposes of classification."
- **Upstream dataset [20]:** "Passenger Satisfaction: US Airline passenger satisfaction survey" by **John D** (`johndddddd`), last updated **2018-06-10**, licence **"Unknown"**. It has extra columns ("Flight cancelled", "Flight time in minutes") that the TJ Klein version drops.
- **Provenance gap (state this in the report):** neither Kaggle page names the airline, the survey year or the sampling method. Some papers add details that are not on the dataset pages. Jiang et al. [22] describe "a survey of passengers who arrived at the airport in 2015, a sample of 129,880 passengers". Hyassat et al. [25] describe "129,880 post-flight survey responses ... US-based commercial airlines". Treat these as unverified.
- **Structure reported by [25] (consistent with [24]):** train 103,904 rows (80%) and test 25,976 rows (20%). Classes are about 57% "neutral or dissatisfied" and 43% "satisfied". There are 14 service ratings on a 0-5 scale (0 = not applicable), plus demographics, trip type, class, distance and delay minutes.
- **Relevance to S6E10:** Kaggle Playground data are synthetic, generated from an original dataset. Leaderboard AUCs are therefore not directly comparable with the published results in 2.3 (see [55] for synthetic tabular data generation).

**Positioning (for the literature review).** On the original data, tuned GBDTs reach about 0.95-0.96 accuracy and ROC AUC of about 0.98-0.994 ([24], [25], [27]). Studies agree that in-flight Wi-Fi and online boarding are the dominant drivers ([21], [24], [25]). Gaps in the prior work reviewed here:
1. Most studies use a single hold-out split and report accuracy, not ROC AUC.
2. Few compare GBDTs with tabular deep learning ([26] is the exception), and none uses current tabular DL (RealMLP, TabM, TabPFN).
3. Only [28] tries stacking, and only of tree ensembles. None uses OOF-based hill-climbing ensemble selection [47].
4. None tests whether the drivers survive in a synthetic (Playground) version of the data.

Our project addresses all four.
