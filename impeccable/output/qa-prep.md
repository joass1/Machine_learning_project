# Q&A prep sheet (5 minutes). Every answer is tied to docs/RESULTS.md (R) or a cited source.

1. **Why not tune for AUC like the Kaggle leaderboard?** Tuning on the synthetic copy rewards generator artefacts. The forum tricks gain +4.7 to +14.7 (x10^-4 AUC) on synthetic rows and lose or gain nothing on real passengers; exact-value encoding is −5.4 on real (R Table 2). Backup slide 28.
2. **How faithful is the synthetic data?** Delays collapsed (91.6 % zero arrival delay vs 56.2 % real), flight distance reshaped (KS 0.22), and a detector separates the two with AUC 0.832 (R 2.1). Ratings are within total variation 0.066. Backup slide 26.
3. **Could the generator have leaked real rows?** Only 17 of 699,635 synthetic rows copy a real row on all 21 features (R 2.1, METHODOLOGY 2). So not row by row, but the real survey is the generator's training data, which is why S to R is optimistic.
4. **Why is S to R optimistic?** S to R scores a synthetic-trained model on the distribution the generator imitated, not on new passengers (R 6, METHODOLOGY 2, 8). It bounds what an airline would see; it does not measure generalisation to new airlines.
5. **Is there leakage in your pipeline?** Target encodings are fitted on the training fold only, with out-of-fold values for training rows; real labels are never features when real rows are tested (METHODOLOGY 3, 4.2). Early stopping on the validation fold makes S to S and R to R slightly optimistic (under 0.0002 AUC); S to R and R to S are unaffected.
6. **Is SHAP causal?** No. SHAP and the what-if analysis describe the model, not the effect of improving a service. A survey cannot tell us what would happen if the airline upgraded Wi-Fi (R 3.2 closing paragraph, METHODOLOGY 6).
7. **Why does SHAP disagree but what-if agree?** The generator strengthened online boarding's link to satisfaction (univariate AUC 0.854 vs 0.833) and weakened Wi-Fi's (0.735 vs 0.758); the two ratings overlap (rho 0.44 real, 0.46 synthetic), so credit moves between them. What-if is less sensitive to how credit is split (R 3.2, 2.2).
8. **Does this generalise to other airlines?** Not shown. We have one real survey of undocumented provenance as the reference, one split and one seed per model (R 6, METHODOLOGY 8). A second real survey is our first future-work item.
9. **How big is the loss from training on the copy?** About 0.0013 to 0.0019 AUC on real passengers versus real data (Table 1: R to R 0.9954 vs S to R 0.9941; utility: 0.9932 vs 0.9948). The copy is worth about 21,700 real passengers (95 % interval 16,000 to 37,100).
10. **Are your confidence intervals trustworthy?** They cover row sampling, not refitting noise. Table 2 uses 200 bootstrap resamples (RESULTS) while METHODOLOGY section 3 says 1,000 for bootstrap intervals; both are stated in the repo. Differences below about 0.0005 should not be over-read.
11. **Why is real AUC 0.995 and synthetic 0.96?** About 4 % of synthetic labels (3.93 % at the 0.95 threshold) are confidently contradicted by a real-trained model, 25 times the real rate (R Table 5). That noise caps S to S.
12. **Why did TabPFN do badly?** Its 10k-row context covers under 2 % of the synthetic training fold; larger contexts or newer weights might change its rung (R 1.2, 6). Weights v2.5+ are licence-gated (PLAN decision log).
